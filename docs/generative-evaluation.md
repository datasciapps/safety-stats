# Generative evaluation

Every other engine in this package conditions on prompts that already exist. This one covers
the case where they do not: the evaluator declares a structural taxonomy with target weights
and a frozen stochastic rewrite procedure, and the prompts are generated by sampling that
design. Path selection and rewrite generation are then sources of uncertainty in their own
right, not fixed conditions.

Use it when the space of prompts you care about is described by a *process* -- a seed question
crossed with languages, styles, wrappers, framings -- and enumerating that space is either
impractical or beside the point.

## What it estimates, and what it does not

The estimand is the failure probability obtained by drawing a structural path with its
declared weight, sampling a rewrite from that path's frozen procedure, sampling a rollout, and
applying the declared outcome mapping:

\[
R=\sum_{h}w(h)\,p_h,\qquad \sum_h w(h)=1 .
\]

That is a probability **under the declared generative design**. It is not a probability over
all conceivable prompts, all future attacks, or a deployment distribution, and the taxonomy is
not a claim to have covered the threat space. Every artifact the module writes states this in
words, because the number reads like a population claim and is not one.

The weights are a declaration, not a measurement. Changing the declared language mix moves the
root risk by about as much as the confidence interval is wide, so the number is only meaningful
alongside the design that produced it. Both the compiled taxonomy and its fingerprint travel
with every report for that reason.

## Declaring a plan

A generative plan is a separate top-level document from the fixed analysis plan. The fixed
plan conditions on a materialized design; this one declares the process that produces prompts,
and one file carrying both would allow an estimand neither engine can state.

```yaml
schema_version: "1.0"

evaluation:
  id: multilingual-jailbreak
  title: Multilingual jailbreak rewrites
  intent: exploratory
  selection_seed: 271828

taxonomy:
  seed_manifest: semantic-seeds.csv
  seed_text_column: semantic_seed_text
  hierarchy: [problem_class, semantic_seed_id, language, style, wrapper]
  semantic_seed_level: semantic_seed_id
  renormalization: renormalize_valid_children

  fixed_levels:
    problem_class: {weights: explicit, weight_column: problem_class_weight}
    semantic_seed_id: {weights: equal_within_parent}

  arms:
    language:
      values:
        - {id: english, weight: 0.4}
        - {id: maltese, weight: 0.2}
    style:
      values:
        - {id: plain, weight: 0.5}
        - {id: formal, weight: 0.5}
      context_weights:                      # a different mix in a matched context
        - when: {language: english}
          values:
            - {id: plain, weight: 0.4}
            - {id: formal, weight: 0.6}
    wrapper:
      values_from: wrapper-arms.csv
      weight_column: target_weight

  constraints:                              # prune combinations that are outside the design
    - id: maltese-excludes-nested-task
      when: {language: maltese}
      allow: {wrapper: [direct_request, roleplay]}

rewrite:
  kernel_id: multilingual-paraphrase-v1
  provider: openai
  model: configured-at-runtime
  prompt_template: rewrite-template.txt
  temperature: 1.0
  max_tokens: 512
  validation: {validator_id: semantic-equivalence-v1, on_invalid: retry, maximum_attempts: 5}
  deduplication: {scope: semantic_seed_path, on_duplicate: retry}

target:
  cohorts:
    baseline: {model: provider/baseline}
    candidate: {model: provider/candidate}
  shared_rewrites: true
  rollouts_per_rewrite: 1
  score:
    name: harmful_compliance
    mapping: {failure_values: [FAIL], safe_values: [PASS]}

sampling:
  method: direct                            # or proportional_stratified
  total_rewrites: 1200

inference:
  alpha: 0.05
  coverage_mode: one_sided_upper
```

**Semantic seeds are a mandatory stratification frontier.** Everything above them is fixed and
read from the manifest; everything below is an arm and is sampled. The compiler enforces that
the fixed levels are exactly the hierarchy prefix ending at `semantic_seed_level`.

**Constraints must declare how to renormalize.** Pruning leaves the surviving siblings summing
to less than one, and a plan that prunes without selecting `renormalize_valid_children` or
`explicit_context_weights` is rejected rather than silently rescaled.

## Deciding the budget first

`sampling.total_rewrites` is the decision that costs money, and it is made before any data
exists. Before committing to it:

```bash
inspect-safety-stats generative budget --plan plan.yaml --assumed-rate 0.03 --threshold 0.05
```

```
   budget  root bound  paths hit  unsampled  rewrite calls   rollouts certif. power
      300      0.0600       62%        109            300       300          0.27
     1200      0.0450       94%         15           1200       1200         0.81
     4800      0.0397      100%          0           4800       4800         1.00

At a budget of 1200, by level:
  level                 nodes  median draws  median bound  worst bound  unsampled
  language                 32          32.0        0.1398       0.1534        0.0
  wrapper                 264           3.8        0.5271       0.9500       15.2

! level 'wrapper' cannot be resolved at this budget: its median node would report 0.527
  against a root bound of 0.0450
```

Everything above comes from the compiled taxonomy and one assumed rate -- nothing is
generated and nothing is evaluated. A five-level taxonomy at 1,200 rewrites is planning to
report a `wrapper` level it cannot resolve at any budget under about 20,000, and that is
worth knowing while the plan can still change.

`--acceptance-rate` projects retry cost and how many units would exhaust their attempts;
`--target-bound` inverts the question and reports the budget a desired bound needs. The
figures assume risk is uniform across the design, so node-level rows show what the geometry
permits rather than what will happen.

## Running it

Two phases, because an Inspect evaluation happens between them.

```bash
inspect-safety-stats generative compile --plan plan.yaml
```

Compiles the taxonomy and reports what it contains -- node counts, terminal paths, what the
constraints pruned, the fingerprint -- without generating anything.

```bash
inspect-safety-stats generative materialize \
  --plan plan.yaml --output run/ --kernel mypackage.rewriters:build_kernel
```

Selects paths, runs the rewrite procedure, and writes `datasets/dataset.jsonl` for Inspect,
`selections.csv` and `rewrites.csv` as ledgers, and `prompts.restricted.csv` -- the only
artifact carrying prompt text.

`--kernel` takes `fake`, `fake:invalid_every=N`, or `module:attribute` naming a kernel or a
zero-argument factory implementing the `RewriteKernel` protocol. Nothing here depends on a
particular rewriter client.

Evaluate that dataset with Inspect and your judge, then:

```bash
inspect-safety-stats generative analyze \
  --plan plan.yaml --artifacts run/ --logs run/logs --output report/ \
  --slice language=maltese --compare candidate:baseline
```

Cohorts are matched to logs by the model each declares in `target.cohorts`.

### Resume

The ledgers are appended as each rewrite completes, so a run killed by a rate limit or a
dropped connection leaves a usable record. `--resume` continues it, executing only the
outstanding units; because selection is counter-based, those are exactly the units the
interrupted run would have gone on to draw.

A unit whose retries were **exhausted** is not retried. It is part of the accepted distribution
the estimand is defined against, and re-running it would grant it more than `maximum_attempts`.
A unit that **errored** is retried, because an operational failure says nothing about the
kernel. Both artifacts are append-only, so resuming cannot destroy the work it builds on.

The plan is re-compiled and checked against the recorded selection ledger first, so a directory
belonging to a different design is refused rather than silently continued.

## Why the intervals are exact

Path selection is independent of outcomes. Conditional on the number of draws that landed in a
node, those draws are independent observations from that node's conditional distribution, so
exact Clopper--Pearson bounds apply conditionally -- and a bound valid for every realized count
is valid unconditionally.

The same argument licenses Šidák calibration across siblings: conditional on the count vector,
siblings hold disjoint sets of independent draws, so their coverage events are independent.
It also licenses reporting only the nodes that received draws, because that rule reads the
counts, which are outcome-independent, and never the failures.

Exactness needs equal coefficients and one rollout per rewrite. Where either fails, the
weighted KL/Chernoff engine takes over and the interval reports which kernel it used.

**Spend the budget on rewrites, not rollouts.** At a fixed number of target evaluations, one
rollout per rewrite is tighter than two by 1.28x, than four by 1.48x, and than eight by 1.86x.
A unit enters the bound as a single bounded observation however many rollouts produced it, and
only single-rollout units keep outcomes binary.

## Cross-cutting slices

A hierarchy answers questions about subtrees. Because semantic seeds are a stratification
frontier, every transformation arm sits *below* them, so "what is the risk in Maltese?" is not
a node -- it is spread across every seed.

The estimand is still well defined, and the draws landing in it are still independent
conditional on their count, so `--slice language=maltese` reports it with the same exact
construction. On the worked example, reading the same question off the tree as a worst case
over dozens of thin nodes gives 0.13--0.36; the slice gives 0.027--0.112.

Slices overlap the tree's nodes, so they cannot join its Šidák frontiers and are reported
pointwise.

## Comparisons

With `shared_rewrites` both arms evaluate the same accepted rewrite, so each unit yields a
paired difference in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). The dependence between arms lives inside a unit, where it
is unrestricted; independence is claimed only across units.

Concordant units contribute exactly zero, which is where the pairing pays: against treating the
arms separately, the bound is 2.0x--9.8x tighter when the arms respond alike to prompt
difficulty, and never worse. Comparisons are pointwise and report the risk difference only;
both arm intervals travel with each one.

## What it refuses

- a plan whose weights do not normalize at any frontier, or whose constraints prune a
  positive-weight node's last child;
- a semantic seed appearing under two fixed parent paths;
- constraints that condition on a level at or below what they prune;
- an identifier containing `|` or `=`, which canonical node ids are built from;
- a rewrite ledger and prompt store that disagree about which units were accepted;
- a log whose samples match no accepted rewrite, or the same sample twice;
- a plan edited between materialization and analysis.

## Reading the report

`report.html` is a self-contained page -- it embeds its data and fetches nothing. Alongside it,
`report.json`, `nodes.csv`, `taxonomy.csv`, `comparisons.csv` and `diagnostics.csv`.

Three fields are easy to misread:

- `estimate` applies the declared design weights; `raw_failure_rate` does not. They coincide
  only when every draw carries the same coefficient.
- `effective_sample_size` is weight geometry, not information. Precision is governed by how
  many draws there are.
- `coefficient_ratio` above 1 means the sharp exact kernel was unavailable. Under
  `proportional_stratified`, a `minimum_rewrites_per_stratum` floor is usually the cause: it
  buys guaranteed coverage of every seed at roughly 2% of bound width.

## Limitations

- **Deep nodes are thin by design.** A 1,200-draw run over a 264-path taxonomy leaves most
  wrapper-level nodes with single-digit draws, where no method gives an actionable interval.
  Read the root, the slices, well-populated nodes and comparisons -- and use
  `generative budget` to see which levels your budget can support before you spend it.
- **The judge is not modelled.** [Judge misclassification](judge-misclassification.md) applies
  to fixed designs; nothing here corrects for a fallible scorer, and judge error is typically
  several times larger than the sampling uncertainty being bounded.
- **Risk difference only.** Ratios and relative reductions are not derived, though both arm
  intervals are reported.
- **No adaptive allocation, importance sampling, or model-assisted estimation.** These were
  measured and rejected; see [design decisions](generative-design-decisions.md).
